A BF16 residual stream against an f32 one, and BOTH against the BF16 reference.

Recorded on the archived perf/prefill-resid-pool lineage through 4d6d15f8 and
retained here as historical evidence. The current integration did not rerun
this GPU experiment.

Eight generation prompts, 48 tokens each, two-node pipe (layers 0:21 and
21:42), one binary, three arms:

  wide          INK_ACT_BF16=0 INK_RESID_BF16=0   f32 operands, f32 residual
  narrow-1      INK_ACT_BF16=1 INK_RESID_BF16=0   BF16 operands, f32 residual
  narrow-2      INK_ACT_BF16=1                    BF16 operands, BF16 residual

narrow-1 is the lane act_bf16_2node.txt scored. narrow-2 is this change.

=== the reference row that was missing is here ===

act_bf16_2node.txt said, correctly, that its BF16 reference row was absent
because `inkling_bf16_stream.py` wants torch and neither box had it. It does
now, and the row costs one stream: 42 layers in 514.5 s, 487.1 GiB off the SSD
at 0.95 GiB/s, one pass for all eight sequences because a stream is per FORWARD
and not per prompt.

So the comparison below is no longer only "the two arms against each other".
Every arm is scored against the unquantised model, on the same ids, in the same
lane, at the same positions.

=== teacher-forced against the BF16 reference ===

The wide arm's prompt-plus-continuation, run through each arm as one uncached
forward with INK_ALL_LOGITS=1, and through the reference as one batched stream.
Every column is an argmax at the same position of the same sequence.

item              n   ref vs wide   ref vs narrow-1   ref vs narrow-2

gen-explain      48      45/48           43/48             45/48
gen-arith        48      44/48           43/48             45/48
gen-code         48      48/48           48/48             47/48
gen-list         48      47/48           47/48             48/48
gen-dialogue     48      42/48           45/48             42/48

gen-recipe       48      46/48           46/48             47/48

  BF16 reference vs wide      361/384 = 94.0%  [95% CI 91.2-96.0]
  BF16 reference vs narrow-1  362/384 = 94.3%  [95% CI 91.5-96.2]
  BF16 reference vs narrow-2  363/384 = 94.5%  [95% CI 91.8-96.4]

  floor: the wide arm's own uncached forward against its own cached
  generation  363/384 = 94.5%  [95% CI 91.8-96.4]

The three arms are 361, 362 and 363 of 384. Their intervals lie almost exactly
on top of each other, and the widest gap between them is two positions. What
this says is that NONE of the three is measurably further from the unquantised
model than the others, and that all three are as far from it as our own two
lanes are from each other.

It does NOT say the BF16 residual stream is the best of the three because it
scored highest. Two positions out of 384 is noise, and reading an ordering out
of it would be exactly the overclaim the floor line exists to prevent.

  narrow-2 vs narrow-1  374/392 = 95.4%  [95% CI 92.9-97.1]

(392 and not 384: this table scores the last PROMPT position as well, whose
argmax is the first generated token. The reference table above starts one
position later because that is where `gen_divergence.compare` starts.)

96.4% is the number the bar was set at. act_bf16_2node.txt accepted narrow-1
at 94.3% against a 94.5% floor -- i.e. the change moved the model no further
than our two lanes already move each other. narrow-2 against wide is 96.4%,
which is ABOVE that floor rather than merely inside it: the BF16 residual
stream disagrees with f32 less often than our cached lane disagrees with our
uncached one.

  first divergence, when it happens: median token 12, range 4..31

The 45.6% is not a capability number and is not offered as one: after the first
flip the two continuations are conditioned on different text and the comparison
stops being about the model. The columns worth reading are the last two, and
they are better than narrow-1's were -- median first divergence 12 against 5,
two sequences identical against one -- against a BF16 KV cache that was accepted
at median token 8.

Eight sequences is a small sample for a median, so this is a "not worse",
not a "better".

=== what this does not say ===

Eight sequences and 384 positions will not detect a small regression. Three
intervals that overlap this completely cannot order the arms; they can only say
that no arm is distinguishable from the others at this sample size, and that
every arm sits at the distance from the reference that our own implementation's
two lanes already sit from each other.

The reference is BF16, not f64, and there is deliberately no f64 lane to
compare against: the host f64 references were removed, and numerical delta is
not the bar here. What the reference gives is the model's own answer at each
position, which is a capability question, and it is the only question this
directory asks.
